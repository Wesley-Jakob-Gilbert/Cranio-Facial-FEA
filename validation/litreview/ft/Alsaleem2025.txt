A BSTRACT Aim: This research was conducted to measure stress dissemination in the mandibular dental arch and identify possible mandibular deformations that may arise from three states of tongue behavior. Materials and Methods: A three-dimensional evaluation of the finite-element model was used to express the tongue muscles’ potential impact on the mandible’s anterior region. The computer-aided design software was used to develop the model. A force of 1, 7, and 500 g/cm 2 , representing normal rest position, normal function, and tongue thrust, respectively, was applied perpendicular to the lingual surface of the anterior part of the mandible and mandibular anterior teeth. Results: A higher von Mises stress, 177 MPa, was recorded in the abnormal tongue behavior state. The displacement detected in all three planes, x , y , and z , of space was highest (0.004, 0.002, and 0.03 mm, respectively) in abnormal tongue behavior and lowest in normal function (0.0000088, 0.0000047, and 0.000061 mm, respectively). Conclusions: The development and growth of the craniofacial structure, possibly affected by the forces originating from abnormal tongue behavior, which could lead to outward and high pressure on the anterior structure of the mandible, with concomitant deviation in the form and position of the mandible’s anterior structure. Four extrinsic and four intrinsic muscles act in concert to maintain their position at rest during centric relation, further guiding their position and behavior during different oral functions. The normal position of the tongue to ensure normal function is on the top of the mouth, touching the palate with the tip on the incisive papilla. Various conditions could lead to lowered tongue posture, like maxillary compression for any reason, open mouth posture, which is the cause or the result of mouth breathing, mal-jaw formation, like an open bite, which may be the result or the underlying foundation of lowered tongue posture.[ 1 , 2 ] The mandible is one of the essential bony structures that are important in articulation. The genes direct the growth and development of the craniofacial structures within the deoxyribonucleic acid. They are then guided and controlled by the environment, which is represented by the posture and function of related organs and tissues surrounding them that act directly or indirectly to control the position, form, size, and relationships of these bony structures.[ 3 , 4 , 5 ] Abnormal oral functions like reverse swallow, tongue thrust swallow, and defective pronunciation possibly impact the growth and development of craniofacial structures. One of these bony structures is the mandible, which is affected during its growth and development by the displacement effect of adjacent developing bones, added to the position and function of the soft tissue enveloping it from the outside, and the probable impact of the soft tissue from the interior.[ 6 , 7 ] In literature on orthodontics, ample concern has been paid to assessing the influence of atypical soft tissue posture with the concomitant function on the craniofacial structures; few studies talk about the effect of the tongue on jaw growth with concern to the mandible, with no exertion estimating the actual impact of the tongue posture with normal and anomalous function. The present study was aimed to distinguish the finishing stress dissemination in the mandibular dental arch, to estimate the possible displacement tendencies accompanying the resultant stresses and to evaluate the infer potential developmental deviations in mandibular form due to simulated tongue force vectors. Finite-element analysis is a numerical procedure that simulates physical and biological conditions. With the improvement in material behavior simulation and computer technologies, this method has become a valuable tool in studying the biomechanics of complex biological mechanical systems. The simulation was performed to observe the reaction of the anterior mandible due to unstable tongue habit deviations.[ 8 ] The Autodesk inventor is a computer-aided design software used for developing a precise and complex three-dimensional model using advanced solid modeling and surface modeling technique with computer-aided engineering to test the functional performance of the design as a stress analysis and motion simulation, visualizing it via software, submitting loads on it, and applying restrictions. Young’s modulus defines the association between stress and strain in the elasticity part of uniaxial distortion. At the same time, the Poisson ratio denotes the proportion of transverse shrinkage strain to longitudinal extension strain in the direction of extension. This effect is due to a higher VMS concentration in that part of the mandible than the fixation points that also showed VMS accumulation, as they are areas of constrictions or points of movement limitation. The lowest VMS accumulation was seen in the areas of connection between the mandibular corpus and the ascending ramus, which probably might be due to the little differences in the orientation of bone trabeculae, where the point of flexion from the horizontally directed mandibular body to vertically directed ascending ramus that could lead to some of the intersection of these trabeculae which may contribute to VMS accumulation.[ 21 ] Displacement of the mandible may occur due to the accumulated stress in three dimensions, as explained by the finite-element model. The displacement in the x direction is represented by a negative value, which means that the tongue forces creating stresses within the mandible push in the anterior direction, that could displace the whole bone of the mandible, especially its anterior part, in a forward direction, like tongue behavior and action in skeletal class III malocclusion cases.[ 22 ] Or it can be pushed, the alveolar part of the mandibular bone, resulting in dentoalveolar proclination like generalized spacing, dental class III, or cases with anterior open bite.[ 23 , 24 ] A study by Uchima Koecklin et al .[ 25 ] on growing rats after unilateral nasal construction showed increased tongue muscle activity that impacts the shape of jaws in all directions. Further study by Chen et al .[ 26 ] showed that tongue abnormal behavior during bottle feeding and oral habits affect dentoalveolar development and occlusal characteristics. In another way, Zhao et al .[ 27 ] showed that possibly controlling the abnormal behavior of the tongue in skeletal class III malocclusion could improve jaw growth and development. The possible displacement of the mandible in the y direction could be due to outward pushing stress from the action of the tongue during normal function and abnormal functional behavior patterns and even at resting posture with a very low level of stresses, as with dentoalveolar protrusion or crossbite. Topouzelis et al .’s[ 28 ] study concluded that macroglossia can influence the stomatognathic system, such as increasing jaw size, resulting from outward pushing of the tongue, which can contribute to breathing and pronunciation problems. Further study by Gutiérrez et al .[ 29 ] showed a relationship between anterior open bite, lateral crossbite, jaw protrusion, spacing, “three-dimensional impact on jaw growth,” and abnormal tongue behavior. On the z direction, the upward displacing stress could preclude to mandibular anterior dentoalveolar tendency for extrusion and proclination that predisposes to class III malocclusion cases or even anterior open bite.[ 22 , 23 , 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 , 30 ] A study conducted by Ngan and Fields[ 31 ] stated that “ Vertical malocclusion develops as a result of the interaction of many different etiologic factors including thumb and finger sucking, lip and tongue habits, airway obstruction, and true skeletal growth abnormalities.” We also refer to the conclusion of Chen et al .[ 26 ] about jaw growth deviation following behavioral changes of the tongue accompanying abnormal feeding habits. Additional conclusions drawn by Gonçalves et al .[ 32 ] and Deshkar et al .[ 3 ] showed the involvement of abnormal tongue posture and function with abnormal shape and relation of the jaws during occlusal development. The results in three-axis displacement tendencies measured in this study agree with the result of Stahl and Grabowski[ 33 ] where Crossbite with mandibular midline discrepancies was observed significantly more frequently in tongue dysfunction cases in a study conducted on primary and mixed dentition age patients. Same coherency of the study results with the postulation of Premkumar et al .[ 34 ] and Koczorowski et al. [ 35 ] Generally, controlling the impact of the tongue on the mandibular structure is clinically possible by achieving a correct tongue stance during oral rest, thereby directing normal function and guiding the surrounding structures to normal growth. One of the myofunctional appliances that can guide the tongue to its original position, leading to normal function, is the Myobrace appliance.[ 30 , 31 , 32 ] Finally, this research study lacks a real reflection of the human structures as the work focuses on engineering a 3D model, although with reflections of the possible texture and consistency of internal composition. The forward pushing of the tongue could lead to elongation or forward positioning of the mandible that predisposes to class III malocclusion. Further, the labial pushing forces, in the y -axis, could add to the class III malocclusion, anterior crossbite, development, or even forward or lateral mandibular shifting. Lastly, the displacement tendency in the vertical direction, z -axis, could lead to posterior rotation, such as in cases of open bite and class III with a long facial profile, as increased lower anterior facial height. C ONCLUSION It can be concluded that the development and growth of the craniofacial structure, possibly affected by the forces originating from abnormal tongue behavior, which could lead to outward and high pressure on the anterior structure of the mandible, with concomitant deviation in the form and position of the mandible’s anterior structure.
